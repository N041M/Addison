01 POSITIONING who it is for, and what it is not

Addison is for people who own a computer they don't fully trust themselves with.

It does the chores — files, drafts, bookings, small fixes — and explains what it did in the words the person would have used. Every action is reversible, and it says so in the interface, not in the small print.

02 PRINCIPLES design decisions resolve here

03 Show the work Steps are visible while they happen, and stay visible afterwards as a record.
04 Household language No tokens, models, prompts, or agents in the surface. Say what happened to which files.

08 VOICE & TONE plain, finished, reversible
SAY THIS
Done. I renamed the 24 photos in Vacation so they sort by date. I kept every original name. I haven't sent anything — it's just a draft. Tell me to go ahead and I'll do it step by step.
NOT THIS
Task completed successfully! ✨ 24 assets processed. Executing agentic file-rename pipeline with 4 tool calls. As an AI assistant, I'm unable to guarantee the outcome.
Past tense for work done — present only while it happens. Name the thing — file names, folders, counts, amounts. State the way back whenever something changed.
No exclamation marks, no emoji, no praise for the user. No jargon: tokens, prompts, agents, pipelines, context. Buttons are verbs in lower case: run, restore, add key.

Step list appends as work happens; live step is white with a pulsing dot. Streaming answer carries the block cursor. Switching chats is blocked while working. Long-running (>20s)Step text gains an elapsed mono counter; a "stop" text action appears beside the live step. Stopping keeps completed steps and says what was already done. FailedFailed step turns #909398 with a hollow dot; Addison's message states what stopped it, what it did change, and offers "try again". UNDO — SCOPE
One action = one turn of work. Undo reverts every file, calendar and setting change made by the most recent assistant turn, as one unit. A restore point is written before the first change of a turn, and named after the request. Undo does not delete files it created outside its own turn, and never removes the person's own edits made afterwards — if it detects them, it asks first. Messages are not undone; the thread keeps the record, with a line saying it was reverted. Anything irreversible (sending mail, paying, deleting outside a trusted folder) requires an explicit go-ahead in the thread and is labelled "can't be undone".
POINTER & SCROLL
Thread pins to the bottom while streaming; scrolling up releases the pin until the person returns to the bottom. Scrollbars are hidden; edges are masked 32px top / 20px bottom so content fades rather than cuts. Group headers toggle expand/collapse; a second click during the collapse animation cancels it. Rails remember their open state per person, restored on launch.
WINDOW
Below 1100px the work rail collapses first; below 860px the chat list collapses too and both become overlays. Minimum supported window 720 × 560. Thread keeps its 580px measure and centres. Work continues while the window is closed to the tray; steps are replayed in order on reopen without re-animating.

BEHAVIOUR
Focus is always visible: 1px #3A3B40 outline, 2px offset, in addition to any rail. Never remove outlines. Hit targets: 44px minimum on touch, 30px on pointer. The 2px rail is decoration — the whole row is the target. Colour is never the only signal: active rows also carry white text, live steps also carry a moving dot. Streaming text is announced once, complete, via a polite live region — not character by character. Step list is a live region; permission requests move focus to the request. Under reduced motion: no scramble, no fades, no pulse — the block cursor becomes static, and everything renders in final position. Full keyboard operation, including every text action; the app works at 200% OS text size with no clipped rows.

17 VISUAL SCRIPTS the identity is behaviour, not ornament

Addison has almost no graphic language: no illustration, no icon set, one accent. What people recognise instead is a small set of scripts — recurring devices that always mean the same thing. Break one and the product stops feeling like Addison, even if every colour is correct.

THE GRAMMAR — FOUR MARKS, FOUR MEANINGS
vertical rail belonging and place — "this row is where you are", "these items are one group" dot state of a piece of work — white and moving is live, grey and still is finished hairline one fact per line — structure without boxes, cards or fills 10:42 monospace the machine speaking about itself — times, counts, sizes, model names. Never a sentence.
Script 01 — the scramble reveal signature

Text arrives as noise and resolves character by character. It is the one expressive thing the product does, and it carries the whole promise: you are watching a machine work, in the open, on your own words. It replaces spinners, progress bars and typing dots — Addison never shows an abstract loader.

duration620–800ms per element, randomised resolve orderleft to right, 75% ordered + 25% jitter · 15% of runs reverse frame38ms tick · whitespace never scrambles poolsone per run: caps · lowercase+<>/ · symbols stagger40–70ms between neighbours · labels lead, values follow streaming14-character noise window ahead of resolved text, 5 chars/tick
fireson first mount, on view change, on chat switch, on a streaming answer, and once per element on click neveron re-render, on text the person typed, on numbers already on screen, twice on the same element, or under reduced motion ruleonly leaf text nodes scramble — never a node containing markup, and the final value must be identical to the value it started with
Script 02 — the rail

A 2px line to the left of things, indented 12–16px. Grey it groups; lavender it locates. It is the only container the product owns — no card, no border box, no filled selection.

GROUP heading owns the rail HERE current row or view
one lavender rail visible per pane · never two
Script 03 — the dot and the cursor

A 5px dot per step of work, and a 7×14px block cursor at the end of text being written. Together they are the entire progress language.

done · #55575C, still live · #E9E9E7, 1.1s pulse stopped or failed · hollow, still writing
exactly one live dot at a time · no percentages, no spinners
SCRIPT 04 — HAIRLINE ROWS & FADED EDGES
Every list is name · value · action on one line, separated by 1px #1E1F22 — settings, snapshots, tools and diagnostics all share the row. The row is the component. Adding a card, a fill or a second border around it is the most common way to break the look. Scroll areas hide their scrollbars and mask 32px at the top, 20px at the bottom — content fades out rather than being cut by a line.
WHY IT MATTERS — AND WHERE IT STOPS
The scripts exist so the product can be honest cheaply: work is visible, place is obvious, and machine facts are visually separated from human sentences. They are not a theme. Do not scramble marketing copy, do not put rails on a website hero, do not animate the logo. If a new surface needs a device the four scripts can't express, it is usually the surface that is wrong.

USING THEM
Dark is the source of truth; light themes swap the same 14 names. Nothing in the app may reference a raw hex. Light themes also apply two type corrections: every text step darkens two stops, and 10–11px labels gain one weight (500 → 600). The logo tile is not themed — it stays #16171B (or #100E09 on paper) in every theme. Adding a token requires a reason in §04 or §17; adding a colour requires this document to change first.
DEFINITION OF DONE
Every state in §11 and §12 implemented, including failed and permission. Keyboard map from §13 complete, undo scope honoured, focus visible everywhere. Reduced-motion path renders every screen identically without animation. All four light themes and dark pass §15 contrast; strings come from §16, not from code.
